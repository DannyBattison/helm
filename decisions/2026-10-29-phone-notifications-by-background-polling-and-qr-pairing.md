## 2026-10-29 · Phone notifications come from polling with local notifications; share codes are scanned as QR codes

Closes the open question in [2026-10-29-phone-app-reaches-desktops-over-an-encrypted-udp-link](2026-10-29-phone-app-reaches-desktops-over-an-encrypted-udp-link.md). Nothing can wake a closed app without an APNs/FCM account and a server to hold its credentials; the user asked whether the app could instead poll in the background and show local notifications. It can, at the cost of delay.

The desktop (`P2P.notify`, called beside `host.notify`) queues each notification for every paired phone, up to 50 per phone and three days, kept in its encrypted `p2p.bin` so a restart loses nothing, and sends it at once to a phone that is connected. A phone's background task runs `collect()` (`mobile-app/src/notices.ts`): connect to each computer, show unseen notices as local notifications (ids remembered so a repeat is shown once), `ack` them, leave. The OS schedules that task (iOS background app refresh, Android WorkManager: about every 15 minutes at best, unreliable on iOS for rarely opened apps), so delivery is late, not instant. Notification text honours the existing preview setting. The text crosses only the encrypted link; no third party sees it.

Share codes are shown as a QR code in Settings → Phone app (`src/shared/qr.ts`, qrcode-generator) and read in the phone's web view with the camera and jsQR (`mobile-app/src/scan.ts`), so no native scanner plugin is needed. The text stays visible for pasting. Tested by drawing the QR as pixels and reading it back.

Why: user request. Not built or run on a phone: the background task registration, the local notifications plugin, the camera screen and permissions. A foreground service on Android would give prompt delivery at the cost of a permanent notification and battery; a push gateway would give it on both platforms at the cost of developer accounts and a server. Either can be added later without changing the queue.
